From the three missions, I believe what makes a route suitable for a robot to share space with people is whether it is able to be short and smooth enough to finish reliably. I also believe that it must do so while keeping its distance from people. For search efficiency, I found that fewer expansions did not mean automatically mean a better route. On the constrained map, DFS expanded only 448 cells but still returned a 50 m path that was twice the amount from the optimal 24.5m that BFS found in 848 expansions. A* also found the same 24.5m path in 728 expansions. IDDFS hit resource_limit at 250,001 expansions on both maps. The successful plans took around a ms to plan so route quality mattered much more than the time it took to plan it. For navigation, the constrainted map showed this best. The BFS route succeeded in 89.3 seconds while the DFS route zig-zagged, ending with a controller_failed message after 142.5s and being 5.85 m from the goal. NAV2 was the fastest on both maps and kept the most wall clearance on the constrained map (.26m). For the social field, A* with soc_2 raised the minimum person clearance from .738 to .794 meters. However, soc_1 used a small .6m influence distance and ended up closer than the baseline of .726m. This shows thata stronger field does not help if it starts pushing too late.
